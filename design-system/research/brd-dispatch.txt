Dispatch Module — Business Requirements
Sutra v3 BRD · Section 1 of the ERP  ·  Users: dispatch desk operator, packer (phone), floor supervisor, accounts.
1.  Dispatch
Dispatch is the floor&apos;s live order-queue board: it decides what goes out today, packs it piece by piece against a barcode scan, cuts the invoice, groups parcels onto a vehicle, and books what comes back. Desktop-first (the desk runs a 24″ screen) with a phone companion for scanning on the floor. One sticky top bar: firm logo, wordmark, dark-mode toggle, LIVE/OFFLINE realtime chip, logout. Six nodes — READY, PACKING, BILLED | STOCK, OUT OF STOCK, SALE RETURN — each with a live count badge. Mobile collapses to a tab scroller and opens on Pending Orders. Every node is deep-linkable.
1.1  Sub Menus
1.1.1  Ready Orders
The “what can I pack right now” board. Only customers with at least one in-stock line appear; anything fully short belongs to Pending and Out of Stock.
Left Pane
Queue rail a persistent rail carrying the board&apos;s sort (default: due days, oldest first) and its filters — due days left, created before, in-stock only, tier, priority, total pcs — with the active set visible as removable chips and a live count of orders and pcs. State persists in the URL.
Main Window
Card grid one card per customer (1–4 columns by width), outlined with a priority ring in the colour of that customer&apos;s most urgent order (P1 red → P5 grey).
Card head customer name, tier chip, market · city, and tap-to-call chips for the customer, transport agency and broker.
Per order order no, created date, due-days pill (red at ≤ 2 days), an amber “n/m pcs in stock” tag when partial, priority disc, optional note strip.
Per line photo thumb, design no, colour dot, × qty, stock tick. No rate and no amount on this board — the floor must not see money.
Footer total pcs with packable count, and a large PACK ▸ button; the whole card is the same target. A fully covered customer shows a green “Fully planned” pill instead.
PACK ▸ moves the whole customer into Packing, flips their approved orders to PACKING, auto-opens one empty parcel and jumps to the Packing node with that parcel selected.
Controls search by customer or design no; collapse-all and expand-all for dense days.
Right Pane
Pending Orders the pane in 1.1.2, docked under the top bar, drag-resizable, open by default on wide screens and collapsible to a rail. Read-only reference while the operator works.
1.1.2  All Pending Orders
Scope the full approved order book — the Ready right pane on desktop, the default full-screen node on the phone: every approved order, in stock or not, grouped by customer, refreshed in realtime. A failed refresh keeps the last good list behind an inline notice.
Three levels customer chip (name, N orders · P pcs, due-days pill) → expanded customer (tier, market · city, call chips, total pcs and ₹) → order row (order no, date, red “n short” badge, priority disc) → expanded order (qty × ₹rate = ₹amount, plus “n of this design is at the last production step” against every short line).
Controls search, sort and filter on the same axes as Ready — this list carries the whole book and must be navigable without scrolling.
Money rates and amounts appear here and on the packing desk only.
Actions none. Approval happens upstream; this screen informs, it does not decide.
1.1.3  Packing Terminal
The packing desk console. A chips row across the top is the master selector — one chip per customer (name, shortfall, invoiced/floor parcels, progress bar, packed/ordered pcs) plus an All Parcels chip, scoping all three zones.
Left Pane — Current Order
Shows the customer&apos;s whole live order book, collapsible by order no → design no → colour line, so the packer sees everything owed before closing a box.
Per line photo, colour × qty, “N in stock · M pending”, a ✗ / n⁄m / ✓ progress disc, and a PACK › button that adds one piece to the open box for items that cannot be scanned.
Main Window — Parcel Boxes
Board parcel cards, each with a customer-colour bar, short code (AA01), status pill OPEN / PACKED / INVOICED, and a PACKING INTO tag on the active box.
Scanning a Scan-here toggle claims the desk for the Bluetooth scanner, arbitrated so phone and desk cannot double-fire. Every scan is validated server-side: accepted, accepted-with-warning, or rejected loudly — duplicate, not in this order, line full, parcel closed, unknown serial. Warnings surface identically on desk and phone.
Per box lines with photo, design · colour × packed/planned, qty stepper and remove, both behind a confirm that warns the pieces become scannable elsewhere; + Add Line; CLOSE PARCEL. + Add Parcel below the board.
Controls collapse/expand all, sort, filter by open / packed-awaiting-invoice / invoiced-awaiting-shipment, and a select-for-shipment mode with a floating “N parcels · M pcs → Create shipment” bar.
Phone companion picker → scan → review → invoiced, with a large tally, green flash on accept, wake lock, and a CLOSE PARCEL that hands the box back to the desk for review.
Right Pane — Live Invoice
LIVE a real letterhead invoice building itself as pieces are scanned — Title | Colour | Qty | Rate | Amount, pcs and ₹ total.
REVIEW triggered by CLOSE PARCEL: “fix qty and rate, then confirm — nothing reaches accounts until you do.” Qty and rate are editable and the bill-to party (gaddi / branch ledger) is chosen here. Confirm cuts the invoice and flags it for print.
Tax the invoice carries a GST block — taxable value, CGST/SGST or IGST by place of supply, rounding, grand total and amount in words — on screen and on the A4.
DONE invoice no, totals, confirmation, Print A4. The box stays on the board greyed as “awaiting shipment” until grouped.
Shipment CLOSE SHIPMENT opens a sheet — consignee selector, pre-ticked list of invoiced-unshipped parcels, optional note — and issues a transport document. Unticked parcels stay for the next trip.
1.1.4  Billed
Sale Invoices invoiced orders grouped by customer — order no, created on, delivered on, delivered-in-days, every line (design · colour · qty × rate = amount), with order and customer totals.
Shipments History one row per shipment — shipment no, date, consignee, transport and broker, parcels, pcs, invoice numbers carried — expandable to the parcel list and re-printable as the transport document.
Filters date range (defaults to today), customer tier and transport, URL-persisted and shareable.
Per order an invoice view with customer score, last broker, last purchase, voucher no and totals; a Return shortcut into Sale Return; re-print of the A4.
Print A4 sale invoice — letterhead, bill-to vs goods-for split, lines, GST block, amount in words — and a separate transport document per shipment.
1.1.5  Out of Stock
Two row kinds everything the floor must source, owed or about to be. ORDER — ordered and out of free stock; and RESTOCK — high demand, low cover, no order yet, drawn from the nightly demand-scoring engine.
Restock rule a design qualifies when it is selling (velocity &gt; 0), its days-of-cover is below the configured production lead time, and it is not already flagged for priority sale. Cards read “selling 4/day · 22 days cover · 18 free · score 61”. Thresholds live in config and tune without a deploy.
Counts strip pcs to produce, sourceable now, in production but not at the last step, and nothing in production — the last in red, linking to Production.
Per design photo, colour matrix — pcs needed, at last step, in order, received, six due dates — with red “Nil” cells, karigar pills with score and call button, and a link to the product page.
Controls sourceable-now vs everything-short toggle, restock suggestions on/off, three sorts, karigar filter.
1.1.6  Warehouse Stock
Three tabs Finished Goods, Material and Work in Progress, sharing layout, search and filters.
Finished Goods one row per design + colour (SKU), reading that colour&apos;s own free stock and in-production rather than the design rollup: photo, design no linking to catalogue, colour, FG stock, in production, status pill OUT OF STOCK / LOW / OK.
Material one row per material + shade with stock on hand, on order, consumed this month, reorder level and supplier.
Work in Progress pcs by design and colour sitting at each production step, with the karigar holding them and days at that step.
Controls search, sorts (scarcity first by default), filters for low only, out only, in production only. Stat tiles summarise the whole filtered set, not the visible page — the board must page or virtualise the full catalogue without truncating totals.
1.1.7  Sale Return
A four-step desk wizard — Customer → Pieces → Preview → Credit note — with the step rail always visible, so the man at the counter leaves with a document.
1 Customer pick the customer, enter parcels accepted (the first thing the desk counts), then multi-select the dispatched or invoiced orders being credited against.
2 Pieces scan the barcode to resolve design + colour at the order&apos;s price, or search a design and tap a colour chip. Repeat scans bump the line; qty is editable, zero removes.
3 Preview design · colour · qty · rate · amount with totals and an optional note. Anything blocking submission is listed in words, not just a disabled button.
4 Credit note issues a numbered credit note in the same transaction as the return, prints it, and lists recent notes as the desk&apos;s audit trail.
Stock rule the credit note is issued immediately, but pieces re-enter finished goods only when the return clears accounts approval; the note carries a “stock on approval” badge so nobody assumes otherwise.
1.2  Cross-cutting rules
Money visibility rates and amounts appear only on the pending pane, the packing desk and Billed. Never on the Ready board or the phone.
Realtime every board is push-updated; a poll fallback and a LIVE/OFFLINE chip mean the floor knows whether it is looking at live data.
Server authority stock, scan validation, pricing and totals are decided server-side; the client renders optimistically but never invents a number.
Roles packers see the floor and no money; the desk invoices; accounts approve returns and credit notes.
Audit every parcel, scan, invoice, shipment and credit note traces to a user, a timestamp and a document number.
