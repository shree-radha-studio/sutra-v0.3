What Sutra should look like

A picture-first ledger.

The cabin side shows a design the way a buyer meets it: photo first, the number as the head, the price in the display face, one maroon line for the action. The desk side shows the business the way an accountant trusts it: ruled rows, counts in the display face, status as words, money only where the role allows. Same canvas, same ink, same type, same chrome. The split between them is density, not style.

The people you named decide the rest. Accountants with two or three years and fresh analysts from tier-2 and tier-3 colleges know Tally, Busy and Excel: they want tables, Enter to the next field, numbers first. The semi-skilled 30 % read pictures and talk. One product serves both when the chrome is the same and only the density changes.

Cabin density

Catalogue, carts, product pages, CRM share, Studio, Home. Rounded cards, pill chips, glass islands, photos at 4:5. This is the final language on the board today.

SalespersonGrid, two rows of chrome, cart strip, island. New customer is three fields. Submit is one sheet.
Semi-skilled workerThree island tabs, the orb, pictures to tap, never a table.

Desk density

Dispatch, Production orders and job tables, pending books, Hub lists, Reports, the Approvals queue. Ruled 44 px rows, 4 px radii inside content, 28 px counts. This is the "structured" Dispatch look, kept as a density of the same language.

Fresher at the deskHome with four numbers and a "needs you" list. Every list a ruled table with search and one Filters sheet. The right pane shows what they are looking at, so they never leave the form.
ApproverOne card per decision with every fact the BRD names, and the actions the BRD names.

Three things to protect in both: the photo beside every design number (it is the identifier), one maroon line per screen (the eye finds the action), and money omitted on the server for roles without the right, so a screen never greys out a number.

The template: what every screen inherits

The crowding you feel is not thirty screens; it is four things every screen copies. Fix them once and every module changes.

2.1 Phone: four rows of chrome before the first picture

Top bar, search island, lists bar, chip row. The first photo starts about 225 px down an 844 px screen; with a cart active the strip and the island take another 140 px at the foot. Every module copies this header, so the feeling repeats everywhere.

  • Three rows: the lists bar and the quick chips merge into one scrolling row. Nothing is lost; one row is.
  • Two rows inside the grid: the lists row becomes the first row of the grid body and scrolls away with it.
  • Call: three rows on every module, two inside the catalogue grid. Decision 1.

The same screen in both densities

Sale orders, drawn once in each density on the same chrome, so the choice in decision 5 is a picture and not a sentence. The Desk version is the manager's view: status is a word, and the approver's flags sit beside it in words; a sale executive would see neither flag.

Smaller inheritances, no decision needed

  • One web shell after all. Production, Studio and CRM wrap the same shell as the finals; only the 8 Sep Dispatch band has its own, and it is already slated for retirement. Clean-up, not a decision.
  • Island per role. Seven targets at the foot for every role today. A packer needs Home · Dispatch · More; a sale executive Home · Catalogue · CRM · More. Decision 2.
  • Two control rows under the header. Many web screens stack three (view tabs, toolbar, chips). Budget two; the rest goes in the Filters sheet. A rule for drafters, applied as modules are revisited.
  • Status as words. Three hues on the sale-order cards today (raised, approved, dispatched). Outlined tags everywhere; colour only for danger and warn. Folds into decision 5.

Entry forms, passed for entry speed

The coverage review proved every field exists. This asks how fast an accountant fills the form. One rule came out of it: the typed numbers are the form; everything else is pre-filled, picked or scanned.

The other three forms

  • New material is twelve fields in the pane. Belongs in the Hub as a main-area form; Production keeps "+ New material · from the lot" on the inward as the quick door. Decisions 4 and 10.
  • New sample is a pane form too. Move it to the main area; the pane shows the moodboard or the last three samples of the category.
  • Process setting step 1 carries nine chips and two buttons on one row. Keep the number, colours and status chips; "Copy recipe from" and "Recipe option" move into the pane's Search tab.
  • Issue on the phone already does it right: pieces to issue for, the lines computed.
